# P24. English-only AI and English-only documents in a multilingual system: clients sign affidavits and read orders they don't understand

**Severity:** high · **Size:** big · **Region:** India

**Who has it:** Non-English-speaking clients (the majority), district-court advocates, firms with criminal, family or property practices outside metros, NRIs' families

## What goes wrong

Clients speak Hindi, Hinglish, Marathi, Tamil or Bengali, while HC/SC proceedings, judgments and most pleadings are in English. Clients sign affidavits and agreements they can't read, can't follow what the judge ordered, and depend on the lawyer's verbal paraphrase, which breeds mistrust and errors (wrong facts in an affidavit, missed compliance). Advocates translate a client's Hindi voice note and draft or proofread vernacular pleadings by hand. Even the SC's AI-assisted Hindi translation needs human review ('leave granted' as 'avkash prapt hua'), and courts are starting to require local speech-to-text for depositions.

## Evidence of scale

- CJI Chandrachud: about 37,000 SC judgments translated into Hindi with AI first pass and human review; AI rendered 'leave granted' as 'avkash prapt hua'. ([source](https://www.siasat.com/nearly-37k-sc-judgements-since-1947-translated-in-hindi-cji-3099056/amp/))
- From 1 Nov 2025 Kerala HC requires district-court witness depositions to be primarily recorded with the Adalat AI voice-to-text tool. ([source](https://www.medianama.com/2025/10/223-kerala-hc-adalat-ai-witness-deposition-transcription/))
- Translated copies were only 6.71% of 34,015 judgments on eSCR in 2023; 2,106 of 2,283 vernacular translations were in Hindi. ([source](https://www.scobserver.in/journal/sc-releases-1268-judgment-translations-on-republic-day/))

## Why current tools don't solve it

Incumbent legal AI is English-first; Indian databases index mostly English judgments; court translation covers selected judgments, not the client's own order sheet, affidavit or contract. No tool takes a Hinglish/Hindi client conversation to a correctly cited court-ready draft in the court's language, or explains a document clause by clause in the client's language, verified against the source text.

## Panel verdict

**Status:** solved (round 6)

**Effort:** M

### What software can fix

Software can give the client a clause-by-clause or paragraph-by-paragraph rendering, in their own language, of the document they are about to sign or the order they received. Each part stays anchored to the English source, is checked by the P23 invariants engine, and is sent only after lawyer approval. It goes over a channel the client agreed to for that purpose, by a route WhatsApp accepts at that moment, and only through P33's outbound guard, under one template contract that P24 and P33 share as code. Before execution, the client can review each factual paragraph of an affidavit in their language and confirm or correct it, as an internal record for the lawyer, never the sworn interpreter endorsement. Software can also generate Hindi pleadings from fixed lawyer-written templates, and stop English/Hindi-only safety checks from silently passing AI replies in other Indian languages.

### What it can't fix

It can't make HCs or the SC accept vernacular pleadings. It can't guarantee the client understood: the log shows what was sent and what the client replied. It can't replace the read-over at execution or the interpreter's certificate. It can't help clients who can't read, beyond the in-person read-out and voice. It can't control Meta's template review, pricing or the 24-hour window. The linter enforces the documented and observed structural rules, but Meta can still reject, pause or recategorise a template, and then that route is unavailable. STT/TTS only work where Deepgram supports the language. Hindi templates come first, and Marathi, Tamil and others follow as lawyers write and approve them. P33 owns the outbound guard's policy (who may be messaged and why). The template contract module is co-owned, and P24 can't send if the guard refuses.

### Why current tools fail and this won't

Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI explain in English and don't anchor explanations to clauses. A ChatGPT explanation in Hindi can change an amount or turn 'may' into 'must' with nothing to catch it. WhatsApp-first practice tools send from a lawyer's personal phone with no consent record, or use the Business API with free-text template variables that staff can fill with anything, including solicitation, and they fail silently outside the 24-hour window. Ours ties each explanation to its source clause, checks it in code, requires lawyer approval and a purpose-specific opt-in, and sends only through one guard. Template names, slot kinds, limits and approved-text sources are defined once in a single contract module that the sender, the guard and the linter all import. So the definitions can't drift between teams, and no person can type into a client-facing template.

### Safeguards

BCI Rule 36: every outbound P24 message goes through P33's assert_may_initiate with reason client_of_matter and the matter ref, including the session route, which has no free-text parameter at all. Every slot kind is defined once in the shared contract and resolved by the server from a closed enum, an org field or approved same-matter record text. Filenames and titles are never used. Templates exist once, in template_registry, seeded by migration with pinned hashes, checked by one linter, and guarded against retired names and drift by a test that both teams run. Sworn documents: the interpreter endorsement is always a placeholder. DPDP s.5/6: a purpose- and channel-specific consent, with the notice version tied to the pinned opt-in body, STOP honoured immediately, and everything audited separately from AI-processing consent. Delivery sends bytes hash-equal to lawyer-approved text, fails closed without a route, and never retries free-form after a re-engagement error. Message IDs are matched through P08's msg_ref on triage numbers. Informational tone, with LEGAL_DISCLAIMER on directive phrasing. PII is masked before the model. RLS by org on all explanation, review and consent tables.

### How the panel's objections were answered

All three objections asked for one jointly stated contract. It now exists as code, not prose: app/contracts/wa_templates.py, version 'wa-templates/1'. P33's guard and P24's sender, router and linter all import it, and no other module may define slot kinds, approved-text sources or limits (an AST test checks this). Its contents follow your preferred choices. Store: template_registry in the database, seeded only by migration with per-(name, language) pinned hashes, with no JSON file and no status table. Names: the four P24 names, with explanation_ready_v1, explanation_doc_v1, consent_optin_v1 and affidavit_para_review_v1 listed as RETIRED_NAMES and forbidden in code and data. Slot kinds: document_title_from_record (closed doc_kind enum plus the record's date, never a filename or title, falling back to case_title_from_matter) and approved_record_media, replacing P33's document_title and approved_document. The opt-in has org_field(office_name) only, and its consent notice is fixed body text whose pinned hash is the notice_version. send_text(..., reason, ref, body_ref) has no text parameter on guarded paths. MAX_LEN is a per-(template, language) table: {{3}} is 350 for en/hi/mr/gu, 330 for bn/pa, 300 for ta/te/kn and 280 for ml, which settles the 350-vs-600 conflict in favour of limits that fit 1024 with the fixed text. APPROVED_TEXT_SOURCES and APPROVED_MEDIA_SOURCES list exact tables and columns. One linter (wa_template_lint.py) adds unknown_slot_kind, slot_mismatch and pin_mismatch, and P33's CI uses it, so P33's lint and P24's rows can't disagree. One shared test file, tests/contracts/test_wa_template_contract.py, runs in both suites. It seeds P24's rows, runs the linter, and calls P33's assert_may_initiate and send_template with references only for every template and language, plus the inventory and retired-name checks. A note to P33 asks them to adopt the module verbatim and amend their round 5 text and tests, which currently iterate over data/wa_templates.json and require explanation_ready_v1.

### Reviewers' minor notes

- Meta rejects template parameter values that contain newlines, tabs or more than 4 consecutive spaces. The approved_record_text resolver and the linter's example generator must refuse or normalise those characters, and the Studio counter should flag them. Otherwise a lawyer-approved multi-line explanation fails at send time with a 132000-series error instead of routing 'unavailable'.
- RETIRED_NAMES is described as including 'P33's old client_explanation_v1 variant', but client_explanation_v1 is also canonical. A name-based grep can't forbid one and allow the other. Keep RETIRED_NAMES to distinct names only, and rely on the 'slots equal TEMPLATE_SLOTS[name]' check to catch the old variant.
- The 'first outstanding item goes by template' rule plus the 23 h 30 m threshold means a client who never replies gets one explanation and nothing else. The lawyer's send panel should say so ('remaining 6 explanations will send when the client replies'), so lawyers don't assume delivery.
- The retired-name grep must match whole identifiers. 'consent_optin_v1' and 'explanation_doc_v1' are substrings of the canonical 'channel_consent_optin_v1' and 'client_explanation_doc_v1', so a plain substring grep fails on every build. Also, 'P33's old client_explanation_v1 variant' can't be listed in RETIRED_NAMES because it has the same name as a canonical template. Drop it from that list and rely on the slots == TEMPLATE_SLOTS check.
- The channel consent notice should say that WhatsApp Cloud API messages pass through and are temporarily stored by Meta. The client is agreeing to receive affidavit paragraphs and explanations of privileged documents over a third-party channel, and DPDP s.5 notice and confidentiality need this stated.
- Migration 016 alters and seeds template_registry, which P33 creates. Fix the migration order with P33, and check for collisions with the other proposals' numbers (014 is already claimed twice).
- Meta rejects body parameter text that contains a newline, a tab or more than 4 consecutive spaces (error 132018). A lawyer-edited explanation with a line break will fail on the template route, and only 131047 is handled. State that template_param_text is normalised to a single line at approval, hash that normalised form, have the linter or resolver enforce it, and treat 132018 as fail-closed with a lawyer-facing message.
- Meta's Business Messaging Policy requires the person's opt-in before any business-initiated message. Sending channel_consent_optin_v1 as a template to a client who has never messaged the number is itself a business-initiated message. Allow the template opt-in only where the client started the thread or the lawyer recorded the client's agreement to WhatsApp contact (source and date in client_channel_consents). Otherwise collect the opt-in in the session window or offline.
- The shared contract test calls P33's assert_may_initiate, and the retired-name grep forbids names P33's round 5 still uses. P24's suite therefore cannot go green until P33 adopts the module. Make the dependency explicit in sequencing: P33 lands first, or the contract module and its test land as a standalone PR that both proposals depend on.
