# P23. Vernacular records need costly, error-prone translation before a case can move up, and AI translation errors are fluent and silent

**Severity:** high · **Size:** big · **Region:** India

**Who has it:** AoRs and HC counsel taking appeals from district courts, trial advocates working in regional languages, court translators and registries, non-English-reading clients

## What goes wrong

Trial-court records (FIRs, depositions, revenue records, orders) are in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Kannada and other languages, while SC and many HC filings need English translations. AoRs pay private translators who are expensive and make mistakes, and the cost passes to clients. A wrong translation changes meaning ('reinstatement' became 're-establishment' in one SC matter). Machine translation errors (an indemnity rendered as a guarantee) read fluently and go unnoticed, and since certification attaches to a named person, the advocate or translator carries the risk.

## Evidence of scale

- SCAORA told CJI Sanjiv Khanna that AoRs are pushed to outside translators who are expensive and error-prone, with costs passed to litigants; asked for an official translator pool and AoR access to the SC's translation software. ([source](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/scaora-highlights-aors-difficulties-in-translating-documents-requests-cji-to-re-establish-official-pool-of-translators-in-sc-286933))
- The SC was displeased with mistranslated documents ('reinstatement' as 're-establishment') and sought a solution from SCAORA. ([source](https://www.barandbench.com/news/supreme-court-irked-mistranslated-documents-seeks-solution-scaora))
- The SC's translation project slowed almost to a halt: only 31 judgments translated between March 2020 and October 2021; litigants from lower and middle strata are often not well-versed in English. ([source](https://www.scobserver.in/journal/the-supreme-courts-translation-project-is-slowing-to-a-halt/))
- Allahabad HC's SUVAS cell reached 1,00,000 AI-assisted judgment translations into Hindi in October 2026. ([source](https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/allahabad-high-court-makes-1-lakh-judgments-available-hindi-through-ai-assisted-translation-554106))
- SUVAS/Anuvaad had translated about 36,271 SC judgments into Hindi and 17,142 into 16 other languages by 2024. ([source](https://www.tarento.com/case-studies/anuvaad-domain-specific-translation-engine-for-the-supreme-court-of-india/))
- 10-model test: only 1 of 10 translated an indemnification clause precisely; one rendered it as a guarantee; AI translations have no independent legal status in Indian courts and certification attaches to a named person. ([source](https://lawbhoomi.com/can-lawyers-trust-ai-translations-a-10-model-case-study-exposes-the-risk/))

## Why current tools don't solve it

SUVAS and Anuvaad translate judgments for courts as reading aids; advocates can't use them on their own records. Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI are English-first. Generic MT doesn't produce filing-ready true translations with legal terms preserved, doesn't handle handwritten Hindi/Urdu FIRs and depositions, doesn't flag operative terms whose translation changes legal meaning, and doesn't tie translated quotes back to the original side by side.

## Panel verdict

**Status:** solved (round 3)

**Effort:** L

### What software can fix

Software can turn a vernacular record (FIR, deposition, order sheet, revenue record), scanned or typed, into an English draft translation aligned segment by segment. The segmentation splits real sentences, including Hindi and Marathi typed with '.' in place of '।', and doesn't break Indian legal abbreviations. Deterministic checks catch the silent, fluent errors: dropped or merged segments; changed numbers, dates, amounts, FIR/cheque/khasra references and section numbers; lost negation; operative legal terms rendered outside an approved glossary; OCR words below a confidence threshold. A named human reviewer must clear every segment before a true-translation export, with a certificate that the human signs. Cost drops because the translator becomes a reviewer of flagged segments, and approved segments are reused from translation memory.

### What it can't fix

AI translation has no legal status: certification attaches to a named person, and the tool certifies nothing. It can't decide which courts accept which translators, or replace official translator panels. It can't reliably read hard handwriting (Shikasta Urdu, faded carbon FIRs): those pages are flagged for a human to type or correct. It can't convert Vikram Samvat or Fasli dates; they are flagged as unconverted. It can't judge meaning beyond the invariants and glossary, so a human still reads every segment. No abbreviation lexicon or verb list is complete. A rare single-akshara abbreviation outside a dotted run, or a sentence ending in an unlisted single-akshara word placed right before another single akshara and '.', can still be split or merged wrongly. The reviewer fixes it with merge or split, and the fix is learnt for that org. Languages without a Tesseract pack, or not yet evaluated with the model, are out of scope.

### What the user sees

Studio has a new 'Translate record' tab. The advocate uploads the vernacular PDF or image and picks the source language (auto-detected from script) and the target (English). They get a two-column view: numbered source segments, with OCR words below 60% confidence underlined, beside the draft English. Segments carry flag chips: 'amount 2,50,000 missing in English', 'negation नहीं not reflected', 'operative term बहाली: expected reinstatement, got re-establishment', 'section 302 changed', 'low OCR confidence', 'segment not translated', 'long segment: check split'. Reviewers can merge two adjacent segments, or split one at a chosen point. A merge across an abbreviation offers 'remember भा.दं.सं. as an abbreviation for this firm'. A split after a word offers 'remember <word> as sentence-final'. Reviewers tick each segment as reviewed. A progress bar shows reviewed/total and the open blocking flags. Export (DOCX/PDF) unlocks at 100% reviewed with no open blocking flags. It ends with a translator's certificate naming the reviewer, the date, the source SHA-256, the glossary version, the segmenter version and a machine-assistance disclosure.

### What the system does

1. Text. ocr_service runs with OCR_LANGUAGES set per job and keeps per-word confidences. The Vision fallback is off by default for translation jobs and can be turned on per job. 2. Segmentation (app/services/segmenter.py, deterministic, no model). Tokens and aksharas are extended grapheme clusters from the `regex` module (\X), with an explicit virama-join rule so conjuncts such as श्री and क्ष count as one akshara whatever the Unicode version. (a) Protect spans with sentinels: lexicon abbreviations (data/segmentation/abbrev_{hi,mr,en}.json plus org_abbreviations); dotted-initial runs, i.e. regex (?:A\.\s?){2,} where A is a single akshara or Latin letter (भा.दं.सं., द.प्र.सं., रा. कु., I.P.C., P.S.); honorifics (Sh., Smt., Dr., Km., श्री., श्रीमती., कु.); legal shorthand (u/s, r/w, w/o, s/o, d/o, Vs., बनाम); numeric patterns (302/34, No. 123/2025, क्र. 45/2024, 12.03.2025, १२.०३.२०२५, Rs. 2,50,000.00, रु. 2,50,000/-, decimals). A lexicon honorific never joins a dotted run with the token before it, so 'है. श्री. रमेश' does not make है part of a run. (b) Hard terminators: split on ।, ॥, '?' and '!' followed by whitespace or end of text. (c) '.' followed by whitespace, in Indic scripts (Devanagari, Bengali, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Odia, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam): split, unless the previous token is inside a protected span from (a). There is no blanket single-akshara exclusion. A lone single akshara + '.' blocks a split only when (i) it is in the lexicon, (ii) it belongs to a dotted run, or (iii) the next token is itself a single akshara + '.' that is not a lexicon honorific. (d) Sentence-final whitelist, data/segmentation/final_verbs_{hi,mr}.json: a closed list of finite verbs and auxiliaries. Hindi: है, हैं, था, थी, थे, हो, हूँ, गया, गई, गयी, गए, दिया, किया, लिया, रहा, रही, रहे, सका, सकी, चुका. Marathi: आहे, आहेत, होता, होती, होते, होतो, केले, केला, केली, गेला, गेली, गेले, आले, आला, आली. A token on the list followed by '.' and whitespace always splits and overrides (iii). Each entry has a source note. Orgs can add entries via reviewer splits. (e) Latin text keeps the English rules: '.' splits when the previous token is not protected, not a single letter or a number, and the next token starts with an uppercase letter, a digit or an opening quote. (f) Split at numbered paragraph markers at line start ('1.', '(i)', 'क.', '१.') and at OCR table row boundaries. (g) Never split inside a protected span. A segment over 600 characters is flagged 'long segment', not force-split. (h) Unmask the sentinels. Each segment gets a stable id and a source sha256. The segmenter is lossless: the concatenated segments equal the normalised source. SEGMENTER_VERSION is stored on the job. 3. Reviewer split/merge rewrites boundaries, re-hashes, clears reviewed_by on the affected segments, is audited, and can add an org_abbreviations or org_final_words row (RLS by org). 4. Translation memory: an exact normalised match with an approved segment is reused with no model call. 5. Translation: masked segments go to Sonnet in batches as JSON {id, text}, with the batch's glossary terms in a cached system block. The ids returned must match exactly. A parse failure gets one retry, after which the segment is flagged untranslated. 6. Deterministic checks (translation_check.py): id coverage; numbers (Unicode digits through unicodedata.digit, Hindi/Marathi number words, unparseable ones flagged); dates; references; sections (धारा/कलम N must survive as s. N, and IPC must not be 'updated' to BNS); negation; glossary (blocking for operative terms); the length-ratio band. 7. Review gate: any change to a target clears reviewed_by. Export returns 409 until every segment is reviewed and every blocking flag is resolved or overridden with a reason, which is audited. 8. Export through the drafting_engine DOCX/PDF with the certificate. Approved segments feed translation_memory.

### Why current tools fail and this won't

SUVAS and Anuvaad are court-internal reading aids for judgments, with no per-segment checks on an advocate's own FIR. Google, DeepL and ChatGPT produce fluent output with no record that every segment was translated or that the amounts, dates and operative terms survived. Generic splitters (spaCy, NLTK, ICU) either break on 'भा.दं.सं.' and 'P.S.' or, with naive abbreviation guards, never split typed-'.' Hindi at है/था. Either way, the alignment-based checks built on them fail. Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI are English-first and don't OCR Hindi FIRs. Here, segmentation protects dotted runs and lexicon abbreviations, always splits after known sentence-final verbs, is lossless by property test, and is measured against hand-segmented gold. Trust comes from deterministic invariants, and nothing exports until a named human has reviewed every segment.

### Safeguards

Every AI translation stays a draft until a named human reviews each segment. Only that person signs the certificate, and the export discloses machine assistance. Segment boundary changes are audited and clear review, and the segmenter version is recorded so a later rule change can't silently re-cut an approved job. PII is masked before the model. Vision is off by default for translation jobs and logged when turned on. Records remain privileged work product: org-scoped RLS, and no translation memory, learnt abbreviations or learnt final words are shared across orgs. Only the read-only shared glossary, seed lexicons and verb lists are common. Blocking-flag overrides need a reason and are audited. Approval points: the reviewer approves segments; an owner/partner approves operative firm glossary entries.

### How the panel's objections were answered

The single-akshara rule (both objections). The blanket 'previous token is not a single akshara' condition is gone for Indic scripts. A '.' followed by whitespace now splits unless the previous token is protected. A lone single akshara blocks the split only if it is (i) in the abbreviation lexicon, (ii) part of a dotted-initial run ((?:akshara\.\s?){2,}, so रा. कु. and भा.दं.सं. are protected), or (iii) followed by another single akshara + '.' that is not an honorific. A closed whitelist of Hindi and Marathi sentence-final verbs and auxiliaries (है, हैं, था, थी, थे, हो, गया, गई, गए ... आहे, होता, होते, केले ...) always permits the split and overrides (iii). That also covers 'दर्ज है. श्री. रमेश', where the next token is an honorific. Aksharas are measured as grapheme clusters with a virama-join rule, so conjuncts such as श्री count correctly. Fixtures added: 'वह घर पर था. पुलिस आई.' -> 2, 'आरोपी फरार है. तलाश जारी है.' -> 2, 'श्री. रा. कु. शर्मा' -> 1, 'मामला दर्ज है. श्री. रमेश ने बताया.' -> 2, and two Marathi cases. A Hypothesis test asserts that N verb-final clauses joined with '. ' give N segments. A gold corpus of 10 typed-'.' and 10 '।' records must reach boundary F1 ≥ 0.97 with no spurious long segments, so typed-'.' FIRs can't collapse into blobs that hide dropped sentences from the coverage checks.

Sunita Devi w/o Sh. Ram Lal'; 'रु. 2,50,000/- दिनांक १२.०३.२०२५ को दिए गए।'; 'अपराध क्र. 45/2024 थाना कोतवाली द.प्र.सं. की धारा 154 के तहत'; 'श्री. रा. कु. शर्मा'
- Splits on typed '.' in Indic text: 'आरोपी फरार है. तलाश जारी है.' -> 2; 'वह घर पर था. पुलिस आई.' -> 2; 'मामला दर्ज है. श्री. रमेश ने बताया.' -> 2 (an honorific after a final verb does not block the split); Marathi 'तो घरी होता. पोलीस आले.' -> 2 and 'आरोपी फरार आहे. शोध सुरू आहे.' -> 2; 'धारा 302/34 भा.दं.सं. के तहत मामला दर्ज किया गया। आरोपी फरार है।' -> 2; 'The accused absconded. Search is on.' -> 2; numbered paragraphs '1. ... 2. ...' at line start are split
- Akshara handling: श्री, क्ष and त्र each count as one akshara under \X plus the virama rule (a unit test over 30 conjuncts); 'रा. कु.' is detected as a dotted run and 'था.' alone is not
- Property tests (Hypothesis): (a) joining N random Hindi/Marathi clauses, each ending in a whitelist verb, with '. ' gives exactly N segments; (b) inserting lexicon abbreviations and dotted runs at random positions never produces a split inside them; (c) the segmenter is lossless and deterministic (same ids and hashes on re-run)
- Gold corpus: over the 20 fixture records (10 typed with '.', 10 with '।'), boundary F1 against the hand-segmented gold is at least 0.97, and no segment over 600 characters appears where the gold has none
- Reviewer merge across an unknown abbreviation clears reviewed_by on both segments, writes audit_logs, and with 'remember' adds an org_abbreviations row; re-segmenting then keeps the span together for that org only. A reviewer split after an unlisted verb with 'remember' adds an org_final_words row that takes effect for that org only
- Checks: 'रु. 2,50,000' and '१२/०३/२०२५' translated as 25,000 raises an amount flag; the correct output raises none
- Coverage: a mock missing id 7 flags 'not translated'; a response that merges 3 and 4 flags both; an extra id is rejected
- Glossary: 'नौकरी पर बहाली' rendered as 're-establishment' raises a blocking operative-term flag; 'reinstatement' passes
- Negation: 'उसने पैसे नहीं दिए' rendered as 'he paid the money' raises a negation flag
- Sections: 'धारा 302' rendered as 's. 103 BNS' raises 'section changed'; 's. 302' passes
- Number words: 'पांच लाख रुपये' gives 500000; 'सवा पांच लाख' is flagged unparseable
- Review gate: export returns 409 with one unreviewed segment; an edit clears reviewed_by; an override without a reason is rejected, and one with a reason is audited
- The certificate names the reviewer, source SHA-256, glossary version and segmenter version, carries the disclosure, and never names the AI as translator
- Translation memory: a re-run on the same source makes zero model calls
- Masking: Aadhaar and phone numbers reach the mocked model only as placeholders; Vision is not called unless the job opted in
- Docker: 'tesseract --list-langs' includes hin, mar, tam, ben, urd
- PDF round trip: OCR of the bilingual PDF with tesseract hin reaches at least 95% character accuracy

### Reviewers' minor notes

- Many typists in Hindi-belt courts type the danda as ASCII '|' or 'l' (legacy-font habit), and some converted Kruti Dev text yields '|'. Treat a '|' surrounded by Devanagari as a hard terminator.
- The certificate should follow the target court's format: the Supreme Court and many High Courts expect the advocate's 'true translation' certification. Make the certificate text a per-court template instead of a single format.
- Urdu (Nastaliq) FIRs and revenue records are common in UP and J&K. State explicitly that urd OCR quality is not yet evaluated, even though the tesseract pack is installed.
- FIRs and depositions in sexual-offence, POCSO and juvenile matters name victims or children whose identity is protected (s.72 BNS, s.23 POCSO, s.74 JJ Act). PiiMasker keeps names, so these names would go to the model. Offer a per-job 'protected identity' option, on by default when the record's sections include those offences, that masks the victim's or child's names before translation and restores them only in the reviewed export.
- Courts differ on whose certification of a translation they accept: an official translator, an advocate-on-record, or a sworn translator. The export should take the target court's requirement from a court profile. It should not imply that the reviewer's certificate is acceptable everywhere.
- Masking runs before translation. Check that PiiMasker placeholders don't swallow tokens the invariant checks compare, such as long FIR or cheque numbers that look like account numbers. Otherwise the reference check passes vacuously on placeholders. Add a fixture where a 12-digit cheque number must survive as itself.
- Indian court tools such as Adalat AI, and Bhashini-based pipelines, already do vernacular-to-English drafts. The differentiator is the per-segment invariants, the review gate and the signed certificate, so marketing should lead with those, not with translation quality.
- Migration 015 is fine, but sequence it after the 014 collision is resolved.
