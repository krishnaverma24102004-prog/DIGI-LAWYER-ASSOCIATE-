# P48. Clients let down by their lawyer have almost no practical remedy, which erodes trust in the profession

**Severity:** medium · **Size:** big · **Region:** India (UK comparison)

**Who has it:** Clients of Indian advocates; reputable firms wanting to differentiate on transparency

## What goes wrong

When an advocate takes the fee and goes silent, the client's options are a slow Bar Council complaint or a civil suit; since May 2024 the SC has held advocates aren't liable for deficiency in service under consumer law. Clients start new engagements suspicious and demand proof of work, and good firms have no easy way to show transparently what they did and when.

## Evidence of scale

- Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi (May 2024): advocates can't be held liable for deficiency in service under the Consumer Protection Act. ([source](https://disputeresolution.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2024/05/advocates-no-longer-liable-under-consumer-protection-laws-for-alleged-deficiency-in-services/))
- Complaints go to the State Bar Council under s.35 and move to BCI under s.36B only if undecided within a year; clients are advised to attach WhatsApp, emails and receipts. ([source](https://nriinformation.com/legal/complaint-against-your-lawyer-india))
- UK Legal Ombudsman 2025/26: 14,259 new complaints (up 37%), 72% of assessed complaints showed poor service. ([source](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/information-centre/data-centre/complaints-data/legal-ombudsman-202526-annual-complaints-data-and-insight/))

## Why current tools don't solve it

No tool gives an Indian client a trustworthy log of what the firm actually did (notices, filings, hearings attended, next steps) without the lawyer writing it up. Clio's activity feed is internal and US-oriented; Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI have no client layer; LawRato rates by reviews, not verified work. Missing: an audit-logged automatic client timeline linked to eCourts events and the firm's documents.

## Panel verdict

**Status:** solved (round 2)

**Effort:** L

### What software can fix

Giving every client an automatic, tamper-evident record of what their firm actually did and when, without the lawyer having to write it up. The record has three kinds of entry: court events (fetched by our server from the provider), system events (the product's own actions: drafted, approved, exported, filed with a proof hash, update sent, quote sent, message answered) and lawyer-recorded milestones, each labelled with its source. Clients and third parties can check the record independently. Every client-facing message carries the current chain head. A daily root is timestamped by an outside timestamp authority. The exported work log can be recomputed offline, including across internal entries, whose contents are hidden behind salted commitments.

### What it can't fix

Software can't change how fast the Bar Council handles complaints (s.35/36B), or the D.K. Gandhi ruling taking advocates out of consumer law. A firm that doesn't use the system, or never records its filings, produces no log, and we can't prove that work done outside the system happened. The log shows what was done, not whether it was done well. A lawyer-recorded milestone is the lawyer's assertion and is labelled that way. Only court and system events are attested by our server. Tamper-evidence is not tamper-proofing. Someone with database-owner access (our own operators, or the operator of a self-hosted install) could still rewrite events and recompute hashes. What they can't do is make the rewritten chain match the heads already sent to clients and the timestamped daily roots, so the rewrite is detectable, not impossible. On a self-hosted install the attestation key belongs to the operator, so court and system labels there are only as trustworthy as that operator.

### What the user sees

Client: after the lawyer turns on 'Share timeline', the client receives the P01 updates and a monthly WhatsApp digest. Every one of these messages ends with a line such as 'Record check: entry 23, code 7f3a91c2e04b5d18'. A private link (no login, no app) opens a read-only page listing, for example: '3 Oct: Legal notice sent (firm system, proof: postal receipt hash). 4 Nov: Complaint filed, CC 212/2025 (court record). 24 Oct: Hearing; next date 14 Nov (court record). 25 Oct: Update sent to you (firm system).' Internal entries appear only as 'internal entry (contents not shared)', so the client can see there is no gap. 'Download work log' gives a PDF plus a JSON file. On a public /verify page anyone can drop the JSON file and paste any record-check code from their WhatsApp messages. The page recomputes the chain in the browser, checks that the codes occur in it, checks the latest daily timestamp proof, and reports 'consistent' or exactly where it breaks. Lawyer: a 'Timeline' tab on each court case or case thread lists everything, internal and client-visible, each with its source label. 'Record a milestone' takes a type, a date and an optional proof file. A 'Show to client' / 'Hide from client' toggle appears per entry, and each change is itself logged.

### What the system does

1) Migration 014 adds the following. matter_events: id identity; event_uuid unique; org_id; matter_kind in ('court_case', 'case_thread'); matter_id uuid with NO foreign key, so trash_item() deleting the case never cascades into this table; seq; occurred_at; recorded_at; source in (court, system, lawyer); kind (hearing, next_date, order, status_change, notice_sent, filed, document_approved, document_exported, update_sent, quote_sent, message_answered, milestone, correction, visibility_changed); summary rendered from fixed templates; proof_sha256; ref_seq; actor_user_id; default_visible, a pure function of (source, kind); salt (16 random bytes); commitment; prev_hash; hash. Uniqueness on (matter_kind, matter_id, seq) and on (matter_kind, matter_id, prev_hash). matter_chain_heads: (matter_kind, matter_id) primary key, org_id, seq, head_hash. timeline_shares: matter, token_sha256, contact_id, expires_at, revoked_at. chain_anchors: day, merkle_root, tsa_token, tsa_url. trash_items gains timeline_head_sha256, timeline_seq, and the new kind 'matter_timeline' for tombstones. 2) Hashing, identical in SQL and in Python and JS verifiers. canonical = a fixed field order joined by U+001F, with timestamps in UTC as microsecond ISO strings. commitment = sha256(canonical || salt). hash = sha256(prev_hash || ':' || commitment). The genesis prev_hash is sha256('dl-timeline-v1:' || matter_kind || ':' || matter_id). Because hidden events are exported as (seq, commitment, prev_hash, hash) only, a third party can recompute every link, and the salt stops anyone from guessing a hidden entry's templated contents. 3) Writing and serialisation: INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE on matter_events and matter_chain_heads are revoked from anon and authenticated. A BEFORE INSERT trigger also refuses any row unless the transaction-local GUC app.matter_emit='on' is set, and that refusal applies to service_role too. The only writer is the private SECURITY DEFINER function _append_event(). It sets the GUC, inserts the matter's head row with ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING, takes it with SELECT ... FOR UPDATE (so per-matter appends are serialised), assigns seq = head.seq + 1 and prev_hash = head.head_hash, inserts the event, and updates the head. Concurrent emits for one matter queue on the row lock, and the unique constraints are a backstop. 4) Entry points: record_milestone(matter, kind, occurred_at, summary_params, proof_sha256) is callable by authenticated users. It checks membership and matter visibility with the court_cases/case_threads RLS rules, forces source='lawyer' and actor=auth.uid(), and renders the template server-side. emit_attested_event(fields..., p_attest) is how court and system events are written. The API process's hooks build them in-process: the ecourts_service.sync diff computed from the provider response the server itself just fetched (never from user-writable provider_data); the draft approve and export endpoints; the client_updates and fee_quotes sends; and reply_as_lawyer. The function recomputes HMAC-SHA256 over the canonical fields with a secret kept in a private schema table (or Supabase Vault) that anon and authenticated can't read, and the API knows the same secret as TIMELINE_ATTEST_KEY. It rejects a mismatch, and event_uuid uniqueness blocks replay. So a direct PostgREST call can't produce source='court' or 'system', and the hooks still run under the user's JWT, adding no service-role path in user-facing code (invariant 2). 5) Visibility: an entry's visibility is never updated in place. 'Show to client' / 'Hide from client' calls set_event_visibility(), which appends a 'visibility_changed' event (internal, ref_seq pointing to the target) to the same chain. Effective visibility is the latest visibility_changed for that event, falling back to default_visible. Court events, update_sent and quote_sent default to visible. Everything else defaults to internal (privilege). 6) Client access is through client_timeline(p_token), a SECURITY DEFINER RPC callable by anon and rate limited. It hashes the token, checks the share isn't revoked or expired and that the matter still exists (not in trash), and returns visible events in full and hidden ones as commitments, plus the head. 7) Anchoring outside the firm's control, in three ways. (a) Every client-facing message (each P01 update, the monthly digest, the PDF footer, the share page) carries the current seq and the first 16 hex characters of head_hash. The client therefore holds a growing set of commitments in their own WhatsApp that a rewritten chain can't reproduce. (b) A daily server job computes a Merkle root over all matter heads (leaf = sha256(matter_kind:matter_id:seq:head)). It obtains an RFC 3161 timestamp from a configured external timestamp authority (TIMELINE_TSA_URL; only the hash leaves our servers) and writes the root and token to chain_anchors and to a write-once object store bucket (S3 Object Lock, compliance mode). (c) The exported JSON includes the Merkle inclusion proof and TSA token for the latest anchor covering the matter. 8) Verification is offline-first. scripts/verify_timeline.py and the /verify page (WebCrypto) recompute the whole chain from the JSON, check that each pasted record-check code matches the hash at its seq, and verify the Merkle proof and TSA token. The verify_timeline_head(p_matter_ref, p_seq, p_head_prefix) RPC additionally confirms that the code occurs in the server's chain history at that seq. That is a convenience, not the root of trust. 9) Trash and erasure (migration 013 integration): trash_item() on a court_case or case_thread works unchanged, because there is no FK. trash_item() now also copies the matter's head (seq and head_hash) into the trash snapshot and revokes active shares. Events stay in the table, invisible while the matter is in trash. restore_trash_item() checks that the head still equals the snapshot's. purge_trash_item() and purge_expired_trash(), replaced in migration 014, call a private purge_matter_timeline(kind, id). That function sets the transaction-local GUC app.matter_events_purge='on' (the same pattern as set_legal_hold and app.legal_hold_change), writes timeline_head_sha256 and timeline_seq to the trash_items tombstone, deletes the matter's events, head row and shares, and clears the GUC. A BEFORE DELETE trigger on matter_events raises 42501 unless that GUC is on, so ordinary DELETEs fail for every role, service_role included. Legal hold still blocks trashing, and purge_expired_trash keeps its thread_on_hold check. Erasure is complete (DPDP s.8(7)): what survives is a hash, which reveals nothing about the events because they were salted.

### Why current tools fail and this won't

Clio's activity feed is internal, editable by the firm and US-oriented. Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI have no client layer. LawRato rates lawyers by reviews, not verified work. Indian case trackers show court dates, not the firm's own work. Ours fills itself from events the product already produces, so it costs the lawyer nothing. Its source labels can't be faked through the API, because court and system events need a server attestation and lawyer entries are forced to 'lawyer'. Its integrity doesn't depend on trusting our database: the client holds head commitments in their own WhatsApp, daily roots are timestamped by an outside authority and kept in write-once storage, and anyone can recompute the exported chain offline, internal entries included, without seeing privileged content.

### Safeguards

Privilege: client visibility is opt-in for anything that isn't a court record or a message already sent to the client. Hidden entries are exported only as salted commitments. Every visibility change is itself logged. Integrity: no role can write directly; court and system labels need a server attestation; appends are serialised per matter; heads go out in client messages and to an outside timestamp authority with write-once storage; verification works offline. DPDP: shares are purpose-limited and revocable. Erasure goes only through the 013 trash and purge flow, using a transaction-local GUC like set_legal_hold, and leaves a hash-only tombstone. Tokens carry no PII. Only hashes reach the timestamp authority. Invariant 2: hooks run under the user's JWT through SECURITY DEFINER functions. The client view is a narrow anon RPC, and the digest and anchor crons are per-org and global server jobs. These need CTO sign-off as additions to the invariant 2 list. BCI: the timeline is factual, not promotional, and goes only to the firm's own clients. Lawyer approval: no AI writes any entry, because summaries come from fixed templates.

### How the panel's objections were answered

(1) 'The client holds nothing that commits to the head', 'rewrite and recompute passes verify', and 'third parties can't check continuity through hidden events'. Every client-facing message (P01 updates, digest, PDF footer, share page) now carries seq and a 16-hex head prefix. A daily Merkle root of all heads is RFC 3161-timestamped by an outside timestamp authority and stored in object-lock storage. The verifier checks pasted codes against the chain history at that seq, plus the Merkle proof and TSA token, and runs offline, so it doesn't trust our database. Hidden events are exported as salted commitments (hash = sha256(prev || commitment)), so anyone can recompute the whole chain without seeing privileged content. Tests: test_tamper_detected, test_offline_verify_across_hidden_events, test_anchor_merkle_and_tsa, test_record_check_line_in_client_messages. (2) Append-only vs trash and purge, both objections. matter_events has no FK to court_cases or case_threads, so trash_item() deletes the case without cascading or aborting. The head goes into the trash snapshot, shares are revoked, and restore checks the head. Deletion happens only inside purge_matter_timeline(), called from the replaced purge_trash_item() and purge_expired_trash(). It sets the transaction-local GUC app.matter_events_purge, the same pattern as set_legal_hold and app.legal_hold_change, and writes a tombstone with the head hash and seq. Any other DELETE raises 42501 for every role. The tests now agree: test_direct_writes_refused covers an ordinary DELETE (refused), test_purge_erases_and_tombstones covers the purge path, and test_trash_court_case_with_timeline covers today's trash flow. (3) Concurrency forks, three objections. Appends go only through _append_event(), which locks the matter_chain_heads row with SELECT ... FOR UPDATE, assigns seq and prev_hash from it, and updates it in the same transaction. Unique constraints on (matter, seq) and (matter, prev_hash) are a backstop. test_concurrent_emits_linear runs 20 parallel emits. (4) Forgeable source labels. INSERT is revoked from authenticated and anon, and a trigger refuses rows not written through the function, even for service_role. record_milestone() forces source='lawyer' and checks membership. Court and system events need an HMAC attestation from the API process, whose key authenticated users can't read, and event_uuid blocks replay. Court events are built from the provider response the server fetched, not from user-writable provider_data. Tests: test_forged_court_source_rejected, test_sync_emits_events. (5) The client_visible toggle vs append-only. Visibility is no longer a mutable column. default_visible is fixed by (source, kind) and hashed. Changes are appended as visibility_changed events and read latest-wins. test_visibility_toggle_is_event shows the chain still verifies after toggles. Effort raised to L to cover the anchoring and verifier work.

### Acceptance tests

- Local Postgres test_direct_writes_refused: INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE on matter_events through PostgREST as authenticated raises 42501. A direct INSERT or DELETE as service_role without the function GUCs also raises 42501.
- test_forged_court_source_rejected: calling emit_attested_event as authenticated with source='court' and a wrong or missing attestation is refused. Calling record_milestone with source='court' in the payload stores source='lawyer'. Replaying a valid attested event_uuid gives 23505.
- test_concurrent_emits_linear: 20 parallel emits for one matter (asyncpg, 20 connections) give seq 1..20 with no gaps, every prev_hash equal to the previous hash, and a passing verify_chain. 20 parallel emits spread across 4 matters give 4 independent unbroken chains.
- test_hash_chain_python_sql_agree: the hash, commitment and canonical string computed in SQL equal matter_timeline.canonical(), commitment() and hash() in Python, and the JS verifier, on a 5-event fixture including unicode summaries.
- test_tamper_detected: with triggers disabled in a raw-SQL fixture, editing event 2's summary makes verify_chain report a break at seq 2. Recomputing every later hash makes a pasted record-check code from seq 3 fail to match, and the stored Merkle anchor no longer includes the new head.
- test_offline_verify_across_hidden_events: from the exported JSON (visible events in full, hidden ones as commitments only), scripts/verify_timeline.py recomputes the head. Changing any visible field or any hidden commitment breaks it.
- test_hidden_content_not_inferable: the export for a hidden milestone contains no summary, kind or date, and the commitment differs between two events with identical content because the salts differ.
- test_visibility_toggle_is_event: hiding and re-showing an event appends two visibility_changed events. The chain still verifies, and client_timeline reflects the latest change.
- test_record_check_line_in_client_messages: every rendered P01 update, digest and PDF footer contains 'entry <seq>' and the 16-hex head prefix of the chain at send time.
- test_anchor_merkle_and_tsa: the daily job builds a root over the heads. The inclusion proof for each matter verifies. A fixture RFC 3161 response from a local test TSA verifies with the fixture CA, and one with a tampered root fails.
- test_trash_court_case_with_timeline: trash_item('court_case') on a case with 5 events succeeds (no cascade, no trigger abort). client_timeline returns nothing while the case is in trash, and restore_trash_item brings the timeline back with an unchanged head.
- test_purge_erases_and_tombstones: purge_trash_item deletes the matter's events, head row and shares, and the tombstone keeps timeline_head_sha256 and timeline_seq. A DELETE outside purge_matter_timeline still raises 42501.
- test_legal_hold_blocks: a case under legal hold can't be trashed, so its events can't be purged.
- test_sync_emits_events: the P01 diff fixture (a new next date and a new order) produces exactly 2 court events, attested, with default_visible true. Editing provider_data by hand through PostgREST produces no court event.
- test_draft_export_emits_system_event: exporting an approved draft creates a document_exported system event that is internal by default.
- test_client_timeline_rpc_filters: an internal milestone comes back only as a commitment. A revoked or expired token returns nothing. Another matter's token can't read this matter.
- test_token_not_pii: the share URL contains only the opaque token, with no phone number, name or CNR.
- RLS test: org B can't select org A's matter_events or matter_chain_heads through the REST API.

### Reviewers' minor notes

- Putting the record-check code on every WhatsApp message will look odd to most clients. Consider putting it only in the monthly digest and the PDF, with a short explanation of what the code means, while keeping the anchoring.
- Effort L for a feature whose demand is unproven: build the automatic timeline and share page first, and add the RFC 3161 and object-lock anchoring later.
- A no-login bearer link to a criminal or matrimonial matter's timeline is risky if forwarded or if the client's phone is seen by the other side. Offer an OTP to the client's recorded number as an org default for criminal and family matters, and use a short default expiry.
- Confirm that the digest and record-check codes go only to contacts with an outbound-update opt-in (see the P04 note).
- Lawyer milestones take any occurred_at. Show recorded_at beside occurred_at in the client view, so a late-recorded milestone isn't read as contemporaneous.
- Court events from the worker's daily sync run without a user JWT. List that emit path explicitly among the invariant 2 additions.
- Hidden-entry commitments reveal the count and timing of internal work. Consider batching hidden entries per day if firms object.
