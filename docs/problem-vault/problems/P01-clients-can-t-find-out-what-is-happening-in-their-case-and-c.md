# P01. Clients can't find out what is happening in their case and chase the lawyer constantly; poor communication is the top complaint against lawyers

**Severity:** critical · **Size:** big · **Region:** Global (UK, Ireland, US data); India (WhatsApp-first, vernacular clients)

**Who has it:** Clients of solo advocates and small/mid firms in litigation, family, criminal, MACT, cheque bounce, consumer and property work, especially NRIs, tier-2/3 town clients and non-English speakers; the firms, which lose trust, referrals and fees

## What goes wrong

After every hearing, clients phone or WhatsApp asking what happened and when the next date is. The advocate is in court all day, the junior is in another court, and the order is uploaded days later, so the questions pile up on personal phones with no record linked to the matter. Answering them takes unbilled evenings. When clients hear nothing they assume nothing is happening. Some then find out weeks later that the matter was adjourned, dismissed for default, or never filed. NRI and out-of-town clients have it worst: they can't walk into court, and eCourts shows unexplained English stage labels ('Appearance', 'Misc. Arguments') that don't say what the client must do. Silence leads to complaints, unpaid fees, clients changing lawyers, forum posts ('lawyer not responding') and Bar Council complaints. In the UK and Ireland, poor communication is the single largest complaint category against lawyers.

## Evidence of scale

- Legal Ombudsman (England & Wales) 2024/25: poor communication was the most common complaint type at 24% of complaint-type selections, then delay/failure to progress 23% and failure to advise 19%; 7,203 complaints accepted; poor service found in 70% of complaints assessed (1,778). ([source](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/information-centre/data-centre/complaints-data/legal-ombudsman-202425-annual-complaints-data-and-insight/))
- Of upheld LeO complaints in 2024/25: poor communication 25%, delay 18%, costs 11%. ([source](https://todaysfamilylawyer.co.uk/leo-reveals-significant-rise-in-demand-for-help-with-poor-communication-and-delays-most-common-complaints/))
- UK Legal Ombudsman 2025/26: poor communication 24% and delay/failure to progress 22% of complaint types; 72% of assessed complaints showed poor service; 25% of consumers got no final response from their provider. ([source](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/information-centre/data-centre/complaints-data/legal-ombudsman-202526-annual-complaints-data-and-insight/))
- Ireland LSRA annual report: poor communication is 'very much at the heart of' most complaints against solicitors and barristers; 143 misconduct complaints in 2020 cited a failure to communicate. ([source](https://www.lawsociety.ie/gazette/top-stories/2021/07-july/poor-communication-behind-most-complaints))
- Oregon State Bar Client Assistance Office (2013 data): communication and neglect were the largest complaint category, at 20.33%. ([source](https://www.osbar.org/publications/bulletin/14apr/barcounsel.html))
- Case Status 2025 Legal Client Experience Report (433 clients, 109 attorneys; vendor survey): only 35% of firms measure response time and 25% track total case resolution time. ([source](https://www.casestatus.com/blog/nearly-80-of-law-firm-clients-feel-uncared-for-new-survey-reveals-major-disconnect))
- The founder of KanoonKit, an Indian practice-management app, lists clients asking about their next hearing date and forgotten hearing dates among advocates' main pain points (vendor anecdote). ([source](https://hunted.space/product/kanoonkit))
- In Meera Devi v State (reported 17 Dec 2024), the SC strongly disapproved of a senior advocate who took fees but didn't tell his client the case had been filed, and raised concern about litigants left without updates. ([source](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/supreme-court-senior-advocate-lack-of-communication-client))
- The SC gave the BCI three more months and asked it to finish complaints against lawyers transferred from State Bar Councils by the end of the calendar year. ([source](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/bci-swift-disposal-of-complaints-against-lawyers-supreme-court))
- NRI forum post: a Hyderabad lawyer took the documents, sent a legal notice, never filed the suit and stopped replying to calls and emails. ([source](https://www.lawyersclubindia.com/forum/civl-case-hyderabad-lawyer-is-not-responding-to-us-152031.asp))
- Chennai client forum post: paid a fee after a 25-minute call; the lawyer stopped taking calls the next day, later asked for more money per call, and didn't respond to a refund request. ([source](https://lawrato.com/family-legal-advice/lawrato-lawyer-charged-big-fee-but-no-service-action-to-take-272291))

## Why current tools don't solve it

Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI face the lawyer only and have no client channel. Clio, MyCase and Case Status offer portals and texting, but a human still has to write and post each update (the step that lapses), clients have to install an app and log in, and everything is in English. None of them pulls Indian eCourts/NJDG hearing data or cause lists. The eCourts app gives raw status, not what happened and what the client must do next, and it doesn't reach clients on WhatsApp. Indian trackers send date alerts, not a plain-language summary such as 'today the judge gave 2 weeks for a reply; you need not come on the 14th'. No tool turns the day's cause-list result or order into a lawyer-approved, informational (non-advice), consented WhatsApp update in Hindi or another regional language, or flags a client message left unanswered and drafts the reply from the matter's actual state.

## Panel verdict

**Status:** solved (round 1)

**Effort:** L

### What software can fix

Turning what the court record already says (eCourts next date, last date, stage, new order) into a short, factual, plain-language update in the client's language, sent on WhatsApp only to a client who has opted in from that number, after a lawyer approves it (or automatically for date-only updates if the firm has turned that on), and flagging client messages that nobody has answered. Most 'what happened today?' messages are about facts the system already syncs. It can also spot silence, meaning a matter with no update for N days.

### What it can't fix

Software can't make a lawyer explain strategy, return calls or care. Orders uploaded days late, or cases missing from the eCourtsIndia provider, can't be fixed by us; the update then says 'order not yet uploaded'. We can't read courtroom events that never reach eCourts (what the judge said orally, the opponent's tactics). Those need the lawyer's one-line outcome pick. WhatsApp template approval by Meta and Meta's policy changes are outside our control.

### What the user sees

Lawyer: a new 'Client updates' tab in /inbox (and a badge on /cases) after the evening sync. Each card reads, for example: 'Sharma v. Gupta, CC 212/2025, listed 24 Oct. Next date 14 Nov. Stage: Misc. Arguments, shown to the client as "the court will hear short arguments on a side application". Client: Ramesh (Hindi, verified +91…).' The lawyer picks an outcome line from a pre-translated picklist ('adjourned at the other side's request', 'time given to file reply', 'arguments heard, order reserved', 'you need not attend', 'you must attend') and can add a note. One tap sends all, and edits are allowed. A second panel, 'Waiting for a reply', lists client WhatsApp messages unanswered for more than X business hours (firm-set, default 8), oldest first. Each has a suggested reply built from the matter's current state, shown as a draft. A third list, 'Silent matters', shows matters where the client has heard nothing in 30 days. Client: a WhatsApp message in Hindi or English, with no app and no login: 'आपके केस शर्मा बनाम गुप्ता (CC 212/2025) की सुनवाई 24 अक्टूबर को हुई। अगली तारीख 14 नवंबर है। अदालत ने दूसरे पक्ष को जवाब देने का समय दिया है। इस तारीख पर आपको आने की ज़रूरत नहीं है। — Adv. X'. Replying STOP ends updates. Replying with a question puts it in the lawyer's inbox.

### What the system does

1) Migration 014_client_experience.sql adds `client_contacts` (court_case_id, org_id, phone E.164, name, relation, language, verified_at, consent_at, consent_method, opted_out_at; RLS like court_cases) and `client_updates` (court_case_id, kind date_change|new_order|status_change|manual|silence, facts jsonb, body text, language, status draft|approved|sent|failed|discarded, approved_by/at stamped by trigger like stamp_draft_approval, sent_message_id). 2) A contact is verified only when the client's WhatsApp wa_id has messaged the firm number and matches (whatsapp_conversations.wa_id = to_wa_id(phone)), or a lawyer records written consent with method and date. Consent to case updates is a separate YES from the intake consent; the prompt is a fixed template. STOP/रोकें sets opted_out_at in code. 3) ecourts_service.sync returns a diff (old vs new next_hearing_date, last_hearing_date, case_status, orders list). A new pure function `client_updates.from_diff(old, new) -> list[UpdateFacts]` produces facts only, with no prose. 4) `client_updates.render(facts, language, outcome_key, attendance)` fills fixed templates from `app/data/client_templates/{en,hi,mr,ta,bn,te,kn,gu}.json`. Stage text comes from `app/data/stage_glossary.json`, which maps normalized eCourts stage labels ('appearance', 'misc. arguments', 'evidence', 'framing of charge'…) to lawyer-reviewed plain text per language. An unknown stage renders as 'stage recorded by the court: <label>' and logs the label so it can be added to the glossary. There is no machine translation at run time. 5) The cron gains a second run at 19:30 IST (`task_case_updates`), using the existing service-role server job scoped per org. It syncs today's listed cases, creates drafts, and posts the lawyer an n8n/WhatsApp alert: 'N updates waiting'. 6) Sending: inside the 24-hour window via send_text; outside it via the Meta-approved utility template `case_update` with parameters {title, listed date, next date, plain stage, attendance line}. Status changes and send results go to audit_logs (new AuditAction SEND_CLIENT_UPDATE, APPROVE_CLIENT_UPDATE). 7) Date-only updates (date_change with no order and no lawyer note) can be sent without approval only if the org enabled `auto_send_date_updates` through approval_requests (new action 'enable_auto_updates', maker-checker like enable_intake). Anything with an order summary or free text always waits for a lawyer. 8) An optional order summary: when an order PDF exists, the lawyer can click 'Summarise order for client'. OCR, then mask_pii, then Haiku with a fixed prompt (informational, no advice), then citation_service.verify_text, verify_output_safety and intake_guard-style checks. The result is shown as a draft and is never sent unapproved. 9) Unanswered tracker: a pure function `sla.overdue(messages, business_hours, holidays, now)` computes business-hour lag from the last inbound client message to the next outbound lawyer/system message. The firm's working hours and the court holiday list live in `org_settings`. A suggested reply uses only matter facts (next date, stage, last update) and is stored as a pending whatsapp_message (existing status='pending' path, settle_held_reply). 10) Silence: matters with no sent update and no outbound message in N days appear in the 'Silent matters' list. 11) Every sent update is also written to matter_events (see P48).

### Where it is built

Extend app/services/ecourts_service.py (sync returns diff; from_provider keeps stage text and order list), app/worker.py (task_case_updates cron; reuse send_hearing_reminders' per-org number lookup), app/services/whatsapp_service.py (send_template with the case_update template), app/services/whatsapp_intake.py (STOP / update-consent keywords, links the conversation to client_contacts), app/services/safety_service.py and migration 013's approval_requests (new action enable_auto_updates), app/services/audit_service.py (new actions). New: supabase/migrations/014_client_experience.sql, app/services/client_updates.py, app/services/sla_service.py, app/data/stage_glossary.json, app/data/client_templates/*.json, app/routers/client_updates.py (GET /api/v1/client-updates, POST /{id}/approve, POST /{id}/discard, POST /court-cases/{id}/contacts), frontend/src/app/inbox/page.tsx (Updates, Waiting and Silent tabs), frontend/src/app/cases/page.tsx (contacts and language per case).

### Why current tools fail and this won't

Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI only face the lawyer and have no client channel. Clio, MyCase and Case Status need a human to write every update (the step that lapses), make clients install an app and log in, work only in English, and read no eCourts data. The eCourts app and Indian trackers (KanoonKit and others) push raw dates and English stage labels with no 'what this means / do I need to come'. Ours starts from the synced court record, so a draft exists without anyone writing it. The approve step is one tap over facts already filled in, and the output is in the client's language on WhatsApp, which clients already use. Because the templates are fixed and pre-translated, the factual part can't hallucinate, and only lawyer-approved text carries anything beyond the record.

### Safeguards

BCI Rule 36: messages go only to people who are already clients and opted in from that number; no promotional content. Templates are categorised 'utility'. UPL: the deterministic templates state court-record facts and the lawyer-chosen attendance line; any AI order summary passes citation_service, verify_output_safety and the lawyer's approval. DPDP: separate, recorded, revocable consent for updates (consent_at, method, opted_out_at); purpose-limited; contacts erased through trash_item like other client records. Privilege and confidentiality: no strategy or internal notes in updates; updates go only to verified numbers; family contacts need a lawyer to authorise each number. PII masking: order text is masked before Haiku; templates need no AI. Invariant 2: the cron is a server-side job scoped per org (an existing listed exception); the lawyer-facing endpoints use get_supabase_for_user. Maker-checker for turning on auto-send. Everything is audited.

### Reviewers' minor notes

- Auto-sent date-only updates rely on eCourtsIndia provider data that can be wrong. Auto-send only when two syncs agree, or when the date matches an ingested cause list, and never for an accused whose personal attendance is required.
- The 'you need not attend' picklist entry should be blocked or warned on for criminal trial stages without an exemption order recorded on the matter.
- Family or relative contacts need the client's own recorded authorisation for each number (or the authorisation of the person who instructed the firm, for a client in custody). A lawyer's authorisation alone is not enough for confidentiality.
- Auto-sent date-only updates must not carry an attendance line. Say 'your lawyer will tell you if you need to attend', since an automatic 'need not attend' could cost an accused their bail.
- The updates consent prompt should carry the s.5 DPDP notice items (purpose, how to withdraw, grievance contact, right to complain to the Board), with the notice available in the client's language.
- eCourts already sends litigants SMS/email next-date alerts when their mobile is registered. Make the differentiator the plain-language stage, the attendance line and the lawyer's outcome, not 'the client gets the date'.
- court_cases.client_phone already exists. Migrate it into client_contacts (unverified) rather than keeping two sources.
