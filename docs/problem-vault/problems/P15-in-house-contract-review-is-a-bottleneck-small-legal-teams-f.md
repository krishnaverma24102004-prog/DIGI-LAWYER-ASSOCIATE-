# P15. In-house contract review is a bottleneck: small legal teams face rising demand with the same headcount and cut budgets

**Severity:** critical · **Size:** big · **Region:** Global (US/UK survey data; Indian pattern anecdotal)

**Who has it:** In-house counsel and GCs at mid-size companies (often 1-5 lawyers in India); panel law firms; business teams waiting on signatures

## What goes wrong

Sales, procurement and HR send NDAs, MSAs, vendor and customer paper to a legal team of one to five people who also handle disputes, compliance and board work. Routine contracts wait days in an inbox; the business signs counterparty paper unreviewed or deals slip. Demand is rising while budgets are cut, and departments absorb it with existing staff rather than hiring, so routine work that went to panel firms lands on an already full desk, and panel firms are squeezed on fees. The few high-risk contracts get the least attention.

## Evidence of scale

- LegalOn 2025 State of Contracting (286 respondents, vendor-sponsored): legal teams spent an average of 188 working days on contract review in the previous year. ([source](https://abovethelaw.com/2025/03/overwhelmed-by-contracts-survey-says-youre-not-alone/))
- LegalOn survey of 150+ in-house professionals (vendor-sponsored): contract review takes about 600 hours a year; nearly half of teams face cost-cutting. ([source](https://abovethelaw.com/?p=1133314))
- CLOC 2025: 83% expect demand to rise; 39% will increase internal workload, 36% use current tech more, 34% automate, 33% use more outside counsel; 54% plan AI within a year; contract management implemented at 62% and planned by 30%. ([source](https://abovethelaw.com/2025/05/interpreting-the-2025-cloc-in-house-survey-results/))
- HyperStart citing WorldCC: the average cost of processing a basic contract has risen to USD 6,900 (secondary citation). ([source](https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/contract-economics/))
- LegalOn 2025: only 14% actively use AI for contract review (up from 8.1%); 79% cite time savings; many want pre-built playbooks or to turn their own into AI review. ([source](https://www.legalontech.com/resources/2025-state-of-contracting))
- ContractWorks survey of 100 UK in-house respondents: legal-tech adoption barriers are lack of resources (36%), not knowing solutions (29%), low priority (20%). ([source](https://legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/half-of-in-house-lawyers-say-companies-miss-contract-renewals))

## Why current tools don't solve it

Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI are priced for BigLaw and large departments; Ironclad, Icertis and Agiloft take months to set up. SpotDraft fits India best but its AI review is generic, missing Indian-law points (stamp duty, s.27 non-competes, arbitration seat). No tool safely separates 'standard, sign it' from 'needs a lawyer' with a verified reason, or takes routine intake end to end: request, triage, playbook redline, stamping and registration needs, signature-ready draft and obligation calendar.

## Panel verdict

**Status:** solved (round 2)

**Effort:** L

### What software can fix

Routing routine contract requests end to end, with a checkable reason for every routing decision. Business users get an intake form. Deterministic triage puts each request in one of three lanes. Green: we generate the signature copy from our own approved template and the requester types the variable values into validated fields; the copy is hash-recorded and nobody's file but ours is ever signed. Amber: anything on counterparty paper, any returned file and any playbook deviation; the playbook redline is pre-made for a lawyer to approve. Red: unknown clauses, walk-away breaches, foreign law or seat, value over the threshold, or missing classification. Legal gets an SLA queue. After signature, a check confirms the executed copy is our generated file, the stamp and execution checklist come from P17, and the obligations go to P16. NDAs and simple agreements are self-served from approved templates only.

### What it can't fix

Headcount and budget. Negotiating with the counterparty. The Green lane is only as safe as the templates, variable types and thresholds the GC approves; we record that policy but don't make it. Most contracts arrive on counterparty paper with real deviations. They always need lawyer time: we shorten it, we don't remove it. A counterparty file is never eligible for 'sign without a lawyer'. Wet-ink executed scans can't be hash-matched, so for those we only help a person compare. E-signature itself: we hand the generated PDF to the company's e-sign tool or Leegality.

### What the user sees

Business users, in a new 'requester' role, use /requests. 'New request' asks for contract type, counterparty, value, our paper or theirs, needed-by date and business owner. For a Green-eligible template they fill typed fields: legal entity name, CIN or LLPIN (optional), registered address with PIN code and state, effective date from a date picker, term chosen from the template's allowed list, and value as a number. A field rejects input that doesn't fit its type, e.g. 'Party name may only contain a company name; words like "liability" or "notwithstanding" aren't allowed'. Status reads Green (ready to sign), Amber (with legal) or Red (with legal, high risk), with the SLA clock and the current holder. Legal's queue is sorted by lane, then SLA breach risk, then value. Each card lists the checks behind its lane, e.g. 'Counterparty paper: lawyer review required', 'Returned DOCX contains 3 unaccepted tracked changes and 1 hidden run (cl. 9)', 'PDF page 4: text layer differs from rendered page', 'Value Rs 1.2 cr > Rs 50 lakh threshold', 'Governing law England: walk-away'. Amber cards open straight into the P44 redline with suggestions filled in, and with hidden content and tracked changes shown inline. A Green request gives a signature-ready PDF/DOCX with the stamp panel, the note 'Generated from NDA v5 under standing approval v3 (GC A. Rao); SHA-256 3f9a…', and a 'Send to e-sign' export. When the signed copy comes back, the card shows 'Executed copy matches generated document (original revision hash verified)' or 'Executed copy can't be matched: paralegal comparison required'. A weekly dashboard shows volume by lane, median turnaround, and contracts that bypassed legal (uploaded later with no intake request).

### What the system does

1. Migration 017_contract_requests.sql adds three tables. contract_requests: org_id, requester_id, type, counterparty, value_inr, paper ours|theirs, document_id, needed_by, owner_id, lane, lane_reasons jsonb, status, assignee_id, sla_due_at, decided_by, generated_sha256, generated_document_id, executed_check. standing_approvals: org_id, version, templates (ids plus content hash), variable_schema per template, value_threshold, allowed_laws, approved_by, approved_at; maker-checker applies when the org has more than one senior. holiday_calendar: org_id NULL shared national holidays, plus per-org rows. 2. Green never handles a counterparty file. It is offered only when paper=ours and the template is in the current standing approval. The signature copy is rendered by drafting_engine.to_docx and the PDF exporter from the approved template, whose content hash must equal the hash recorded in the standing approval, together with the variable values typed into our form. 3. Variable validation is code in app/services/template_variables.py. Each [●…●] slot in an approved template declares a type: entity_name (max 120 chars; letters, digits, spaces and & . , ' ( ) -; must end in a recognised entity suffix such as Private Limited, Pvt Ltd, LLP, Limited or a sole-proprietor marker; no digits except within a CIN field), cin/llpin/gstin (format regex plus GSTIN check digit), indian_address (max 250 chars, a six-digit PIN code, state from the fixed list), date (ISO, from a picker, within ±2 years), enum (template-listed options only), and inr_amount (number, min/max). Every free-text type rejects a blocklist of clause words: shall, notwithstanding, liabilit*, indemn*, clause, cl., section, governing, arbitrat*, terminat*, waive*, unlimited, exclusive, plus newlines and control characters. Rejected input blocks Green, and the requester may send the request to Amber with the text attached. 4. When the file is generated, its SHA-256 is stored. The DOCX is built with no comments, revisions, hidden runs, fields, embedded objects or altChunks, and a unit test inspects every OOXML part of the output to prove it. 5. Triage order, all in code: (a) paper=theirs, or any uploaded counterparty file, goes to Amber at best. (b) The full-fidelity file inspector (app/services/file_inspector.py) runs on every uploaded file. For DOCX it unzips all parts and checks document, headers, footers, footnotes, endnotes, comments, glossary and embeddings. It flags w:ins, w:del, w:moveFrom/To, w:vanish, w:specVanish, white or 1-pt text, w:altChunk, OLE objects, external relationships and macros (vbaProject). The text of every part is extracted, so contract_diff_service compares headers, footers, footnotes and schedules too. For PDF it checks invisible text (render mode 3), text drawn in the background colour, pages without a text layer, and annotations or form fields. It also renders each page with pypdfium2, OCRs it, and compares the result with the text layer; a page below 0.9 similarity is flagged. Any flag is shown to the lawyer as a risk line, and the request can't drop below Amber. (c) governing_law.detect must return a law in the allowed list. (d) The value must be under the threshold; the value parsed from the document by code must equal the stated value, or the request goes to Amber. (e) A P17 stamp assessment must exist and be confirmed. (f) No P44 walk-away rule may fire. Green requires paper=ours plus all of (c) to (f). Any walk-away rule, unknown topic, foreign law or seat, or over-threshold value gives Red. Everything else is Amber. The model is never asked whether a document is safe to sign. 6. Signature verification: when the executed copy is uploaded, we check that it is our file. For an e-signed PDF, code locates the original revision (the bytes before the first incremental update, found from the signature /ByteRange and %%EOF offsets) and compares its SHA-256 with generated_sha256. A match sets executed_check='verified'. Any other file, such as a wet-ink scan or a re-saved PDF, gets executed_check='unverified'. For those we OCR the scan, run contract_diff against the generated text and the file inspector, and a paralegal or lawyer must tick 'compared'; a diff in any clause escalates the request to Amber after signature, and legal is alerted. 7. Amber and Red: the P44 redline runs automatically and the assigned lawyer accepts or rejects. The final DOCX is generated only after approval (drafts.approved_by), and any edit clears the approval. 8. SLA timers and reassignment are plain rules over holiday_calendar. Reminders reuse the worker cron. 9. Email-in through the P42 connector (DMARC-aligned only) creates a draft request with the attachments; those are counterparty files, so the request is Amber at best.

### Why current tools fail and this won't

Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI are built and priced for BigLaw research and drafting, not for the intake queue of a two-lawyer legal team. Ironclad and Icertis take months to configure. SpotDraft's AI review is generic on Indian points. CLMs that auto-approve on a text diff can be beaten by hidden runs, tracked changes or a doctored PDF text layer, because they compare extracted text, not the file that gets signed. Our Green lane has a reason a GC can defend: the file signed is the one we generated, from a hash-pinned template, with typed and validated values, under a named standing approval, and the hash is checked again on the executed copy. Every counterparty file goes to a lawyer, with hidden content surfaced and the playbook redline already made.

### Safeguards

No counterparty file is ever signed without a lawyer: Green signs only our generated file from a hash-pinned template under a named standing approval. Free-text slots can't carry clause language. The executed copy is hash-verified or compared by a person. No AI-written text is in a Green file, and AI-suggested redlines reach the counterparty only after a lawyer approves them. The requester role has least privilege under RLS. PII is masked before anything goes to Claude (the Green path makes no Claude call). Lane decisions, standing-approval changes, variable rejections and executed-copy checks are audit-logged. In a law-firm deployment (a panel firm running a client's contract desk), the firm's advocate is the approver, which keeps the BCI position clear. In-house, the company's legal team is responsible for its standing approval.

### How the panel's objections were answered

All three objections say Green can't rest on a text diff of a counterparty file. Fixed. (1) Green never uses a counterparty file. It exists only for our paper, and the signature copy is regenerated from a template whose hash is pinned in the standing approval, filled with values typed into our form. contract_diff_service is now only a review aid for lawyers. (2) Variable-slot injection: template_variables.py gives every slot a type (entity_name, cin/gstin, indian_address, date, enum, inr_amount) with length, character and format limits and a blocklist of clause words. 'Beta LLP, notwithstanding clause 9…' is rejected, and a test covers it. (3) Hidden content: the new file_inspector.py inspects every OOXML part (headers, footers, footnotes, endnotes, comments, embeddings). It rejects w:ins/w:del/move, w:vanish/specVanish, white or tiny text, altChunk, OLE objects and macros. For PDFs it flags invisible text, background-colour text, image-only pages, and pages whose rendered OCR differs from the text layer. Any flag keeps a request at Amber or higher. Fixtures for hidden runs, unaccepted tracked changes, footers, footnotes, comments, OLE objects and a doctored PDF layer must never be Green. (4) What is signed is what we generated. generated_sha256 is recorded, and after e-signature the PDF's original revision is hash-matched. Wet-ink or re-saved copies are 'unverified' and need a recorded comparison by a person; a diff escalates after signature. (5) DOCX extraction is added in document_service (shared with P42), since the current extractor rejects DOCX.

### Acceptance tests

- tests/test_template_variables.py: 'Beta LLP' passes entity_name; 'Beta LLP, notwithstanding clause 9 whose liability shall be unlimited' is rejected and names the blocked words; a newline in an address is rejected; an address without a six-digit PIN code is rejected; an invalid GSTIN check digit is rejected.
- tests/test_triage.py: an NDA v5 request on our paper with valid typed variables, confirmed stamp, Indian law and value under the threshold gives Green; its generated DOCX has zero w:ins/w:del/w:vanish/comments/embeddings in every part, and generated_sha256 is stored.
- A counterparty-returned DOCX whose visible text is identical to the approved NDA v5 is Amber, never Green, with reason 'counterparty file'.
- Fixture with a hidden run (w:vanish) adding 'liability unlimited' in cl. 9: Amber, and the flag names cl. 9 and quotes the hidden text.
- Fixture with an unaccepted w:ins in cl. 15, one with a changed footer, one with a footnote, one with a comment and one with an embedded OLE object: each is Amber with its own flag.
- PDF fixture whose text layer says 'Indian law' while the rendered page image says 'English law' (white overlay): the page is flagged as a rendered-vs-text mismatch and the request is not Green. PDF with a page lacking a text layer: flagged.
- Template whose content hash differs from the hash in the standing approval: Green is disabled with reason 'template changed since approval'.
- Governing law Singapore gives Red even when a mocked Claude says it's fine. Stated value Rs 20 lakh but document says Rs 2 crore gives Amber with a value mismatch reason. No confirmed stamp assessment gives Amber with reason 'stamp not computed'.
- Green is disabled when standing_approvals has no current version or when the GC toggles it off.
- tests/test_signature_verify.py: our generated PDF signed with an incremental update (fixture made with pyHanko) verifies against generated_sha256. The same PDF with one byte changed in the original revision, or re-saved without the incremental structure, gives 'unverified' and a paralegal task.
- A wet-ink scan whose cl. 9 differs from the generated text: after signature, escalates to Amber and alerts legal.
- The final DOCX endpoint returns 409 for an Amber request until the assigned lawyer approves; any edit after approval clears it.
- tests/db/test_requester_rls.py: a requester sees only their own requests and their department's contract summary fields, never legal's notes, redline explanations or other departments' contracts.
- SLA: a request created Friday 18:00 with a 2-business-day SLA is due Tuesday 18:00, with holidays in holiday_calendar excluded, checked with a frozen clock.

### Reviewers' minor notes

- Green requires a confirmed P17 stamp assessment, but the requester is a business user. Pin the instrument key and state per template in the standing approval, so a non-lawyer never confirms a stamp classification.
- entity_name rejects legitimate counterparties: names with digits ('3M India Limited'), partnership firms, trusts, societies, government bodies and individuals. Add those types, or route them to Amber with a clear reason instead of a hard rejection.
- Many e-sign tools (DocuSign, some Aadhaar eSign vendors) flatten and re-save, or append certificate pages, so the original-revision hash will often fail. Expect the paralegal comparison path to be the common case, and size the workload accordingly.
- Green requires a 'confirmed' P17 stamp assessment, but P17 lets 'the user' confirm. A business requester should not decide stamp classification. Pin the instrument key and state per template in the standing approval, or require a lawyer to confirm.
- In a law-firm deployment, the standing approval should record the approving advocate's name and enrolment number, and an engagement with the client company. That evidences that the firm advised on the template and is not offering software-delivered advice to non-clients.
- Migration 017 collides with P46.
- The entity_name blocklist pattern 'liabilit*' rejects the full form 'XYZ Limited Liability Partnership'. Strip recognised entity suffixes before applying the clause-word blocklist, and add a test.
- entity_name requires a corporate or proprietor suffix. Individuals, partnership firms, trusts, societies and government bodies need their own typed slots, or they will always fall to Amber.
- Most Indian e-sign or e-stamp flows (Leegality, stamp page prepended) rewrite the PDF instead of appending an incremental update. Expect 'unverified' to be the common case and size the paralegal comparison work accordingly.
- Depends on P44 and P17 modules, which aren't in this batch. Migration 017 collides with P46.
