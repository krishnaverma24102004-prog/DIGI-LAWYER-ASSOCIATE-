# P04. Adjournment churn and counsel double-booking: 66.6 lakh district-court cases delayed because the lawyer isn't available, and clients lose a day's wages each time

**Severity:** critical · **Size:** big · **Region:** India

**Who has it:** Busy trial-court and High Court litigators, chambers of busy seniors, district-court advocates with 50-plus matters a day, proxy juniors, and litigants (especially daily-wage and salaried clients)

## What goes wrong

An advocate has five matters in three courtrooms or two court complexes on the same morning. Clashes are found that morning or not at all. While counsel argues one matter, the others are adjourned, passed over, or handled by a proxy junior who seeks a date without knowing the file and is briefed over WhatsApp. Each adjournment means another hearing, another client trip, more risk of ex parte orders and costs. The client takes leave, travels and waits all day for nothing ('tareekh pe tareekh'), paying lost income plus the per-appearance fee, with no warning that their lawyer had a clash. NJDG records 'counsel not available' as the single largest category of delay in district courts.

## Evidence of scale

- NJDG as of 14 Sep 2024: 66,59,565 district-court cases delayed because lawyers were unavailable, against over 38 lakh for absconding accused, 29,20,033 for witnesses and 24,62,051 stayed. ([source](https://barandbench.com/news/66-lakh-cases-district-courts-delayed-unavailability-lawyers))
- CJI D Y Chandrachud (Dec 2022): NJDG shows over 63 lakh cases delayed for non-availability of counsel; he asked for the Bar's support. ([source](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/national-judicial-database-says-63-lakh-cases-considered-delayed-due-to-non-availability-of-counsels-cji-news-249581))
- Odisha (NJDG): more than 4.61 lakh cases pending for non-availability of counsel, alongside more than 16 lakh pending in district and lower courts. ([source](https://www.orissapost.com/over-16-lakh-cases-pending-in-district-lower-courts-1l-in-hc-in-odisha-njdg-data/amp/))
- As of 31 Dec 2025 (NJDG, Law Ministry in Parliament): 4,76,57,328 cases pending in district and subordinate courts, 63,66,023 in the High Courts and 92,101 in the Supreme Court. ([source](https://ianslive.in/over-476-crore-cases-pending-across-indian-courts-govt-tells-parliament--20260213184542))
- DAKSH (2015): 20 to 68 days on average between scheduled hearings of the same case across the High Courts studied (about 25 in Calcutta, 61 in Delhi, 68 in Karnataka). ([source](https://www.legallyindia.com/the-bar-and-bench/daksh-cases-speed-high-courts-20150827-6504))
- DAKSH survey of 9,329 litigants: average spend of Rs 520 a day to attend court; lost wages and business income equal 0.48% of GDP; litigants earning under Rs 1 lakh a year spend 25% of their earnings attending hearings. ([source](https://www.dakshindia.org/access-to-justice-survey-findings-india))
- DAKSH Access to Justice survey (9,329 litigants, 305 locations, 24 states): about Rs 30,000 crore a year in direct litigation expenses plus Rs 50,387 crore in lost wages and business income. ([source](https://countercurrents.org/2026/10/the-invisible-queue-why-millions-of-indians-walk-away-before-justice-even-begins/))
- DAKSH (2016): lost wages and legal fees cost litigants over Rs 80,000 crore a year. ([source](https://www.dakshindia.org/2016/04/21/))

## Why current tools don't solve it

Clio and US docketing tools assume one court calendar per matter and US rules. Indian diaries and trackers (eCourts app, per-case and per-court) list next dates but have no advocate-level, firm-wide clash view across district courts, High Courts and tribunals, no sense of when an item in a given courtroom is likely to be reached, and no automatic proxy brief (last order, stage, what to seek and not concede). Nothing warns the client not to travel. Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI have no scheduling layer.

## Panel verdict

**Status:** solved (round 3)

**Effort:** M

### What software can fix

Spotting clashes the evening before instead of during the morning. That means a firm-wide view of each advocate's listings across courtrooms and complexes, with reach estimates that every logged call improves, keyed to the bench actually sitting. Real overlaps are kept apart from mornings where the data is thin. The system suggests a reassignment first, gives the proxy a brief, counts adjournments per party, and offers a client message only where the record supports one, worded so it never predicts the court's decision.

### What it can't fix

Out of scope: judges' board management, courts not reaching their lists, the shortage of judges and the culture of seeking dates. Reach estimates are statistics from our own appearance logs. Courts have not endorsed them and they are never put to the court as a ground. A clash is not a ground for adjournment (Order XVII Rule 1 proviso (b) CPC), and the product cannot make a court grant a pass-over. A pace model can't foresee a long part-heard matter, a judge rising early or out-of-turn mentions, so windows are ranges, not promises. In the first weeks most courtrooms have no data and the board says so (amber) instead of guessing. We can't stop a client travelling if the firm chooses not to message them. We can't know about a bench change before the cause list or roster is published.

### What the user sees

On the board (P05), each advocate's morning is a strip showing courtroom, bench ('Justice A / Justice A & Justice B'), item number and its position in the day's list ('item 8, 14th call'), the estimated reach window and travel time between complexes. A window reads 'usually reached 11:15-12:30 before this bench (pace from 34 calls at items 2-41, this roster)'. Thin data reads 'new bench since 14 Oct: not enough data (9 of 20)'. History carried from another courtroom reads 'carried from Justice A's earlier court, low confidence'. There are two clash states. A red OVERLAP appears only when both items have estimated windows and the padded windows overlap: 'Court 12 item 8 (11:15-12:30) and Court 31 item 5 (11:40-12:10) overlap; 25 min apart'. Red items get the fix list. An amber POSSIBLE CLASH, NO DATA appears when at least one courtroom lacks a window. It is shown once per advocate-morning ('2 courtrooms without reach data today'), not as a pair-by-pair alarm, and offers no reassignment unless the advocate ticks 'treat as clash' for that day or sets it as a standing preference. Items in the same courtroom never clash. Fixes come in a fixed order: (1) reassign to an available colleague who knows the matter; (2) only if nobody is free, a 'seek pass-over / keep back' note for the appearing counsel. The system never offers 'seek adjournment because counsel is engaged elsewhere'. Proxy brief, adjournment counter and client-message button are unchanged from the last round.

### What the system does

1. Positions (deterministic): for each courtroom and date the P05 parser gives the printed cause list in order, with each item's segment label (fresh / after notice / regular / supplementary / advance, normalised per court profile). An item's position P is its rank in the full printed order for that courtroom and day. Connected matters listed 'with' an item count as one call, and deleted or 'not to be taken up' items are skipped.
2. Sitting clock: court timings are data per complex (sitting start, lunch interval, rising time, e.g. Delhi HC 10:30, lunch 13:00-14:15). The day's sitting start is the junior's 'bench assembled' tap if logged, otherwise the profile time. Elapsed time is counted in sitting minutes, which exclude the lunch interval.
3. Observations: every 'called' tap (first call only; a pass-over and recall keeps the first) stores bench_key, roster_period_id, segment label, position P and elapsed sitting minutes E. Its pace is r = E / max(P, 3); the floor of 3 stops item-1 noise from dominating. Every tap at any item number therefore informs every item in that (bench, segment).
4. Weights (as last round): current roster period 1.0; the same bench in earlier periods 0.5^(periods back), labelled 'carried'; other benches 0; history of the same judge(s) from another courtroom is a labelled low-confidence hint with weight 0 toward the threshold. An unparsed header gives bench_key null and 'bench unknown'.
5. Estimator: for (bench_key, segment) with effective weight W >= 20, compute the weighted 25th and 75th percentiles of r. The weighted quantile q is the smallest r whose cumulative normalised weight, sorted ascending, is >= q; there is no interpolation, so the result is reproducible exactly. The window for a target item at position P is [start + q25 x max(P,3), start + q75 x max(P,3)] in sitting minutes, mapped back to clock time by adding the lunch interval where crossed, and capped at rising time ('may not be reached today' if the lower bound passes rising). When a segment has W < 20 but the bench's pooled segments have W >= 20, the pooled pace is used, labelled 'pooled across list types'. Otherwise the window is 'not enough data'. A firm that logs 3 calls a day in a courtroom crosses 20 in about a week.
6. Reach frequency (display only): from 'court rose at item K' taps, the share of current-roster sittings that reached position P ('reached in 7 of 9 sittings'). It is never used for clash colouring.
7. Clash classification (deterministic): for each advocate and date, take the pairs of items in different courtrooms. A pair is OVERLAP (red) iff both windows exist and [lo-travel, hi+travel] intersect, using the firm-maintained complex-to-complex travel matrix (same complex, different courtroom: a default of 5 minutes). It is POSSIBLE_NO_DATA (amber) iff either window is missing. Amber pairs are collapsed into one advocate-morning notice. Only red items, or amber ones where the advocate opted in, enter the resolution step.
8. Resolution order, in code (unchanged): reassignment candidates first, meaning colleagues free in the padded window (a colleague with an amber item at that time counts as free only if that item is in the same complex), of adequate seniority for the stage, enrolled in the roster, ranked by prior appearance in the matter. Only when the list is empty is the fixed pass-over note offered, never an adjournment request.
9. Proxy brief, adjournment counter and client-message gate are unchanged from round 2 (quote-verified AI summary of the last order; Order XVII Rule 1 / s.346 BNSS counts; message suppressed for an accused without an exemption, a presence direction or a summoned witness; reason taken from the record; lawyer's click; audit-logged). No AI anywhere in steps 1-8.

### Why current tools fail and this won't

Clio and US docketing tools assume one calendar per matter and US rules. eCourts and Indian trackers (eCourts app, eCourtsIndia API, LawStar-type diaries) work per case and per court. They don't show one advocate's clashes and don't know which bench is sitting. Harvey, CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI have no scheduling layer. Our estimate is a defined pace model per bench and list segment, which every call at any item number feeds, so it becomes useful within about a week. A roster change resets it. Thin data shows as one amber notice instead of a red alarm. Clashes are resolved by reassignment before anything reaches the court, and the client message is gated by the record, not by a prediction.

### Safeguards

Client messages need a lawyer's click and a reason taken from the record. They are suppressed for an accused without an exemption and wherever an order requires presence. They never predict the outcome, and they go only to the firm's existing clients with recorded consent, so there is no solicitation under BCI Rule 36. Nothing produced for the court seeks an adjournment because counsel has a clash. Proxies are assigned only to enrolled advocates in the roster. Order text is masked before AI, and the brief is internal work product. Reach statistics stay internal and are labelled with their observation count and roster period. Amber notices never generate work unless the advocate opts in.

### How the panel's objections were answered

Objection 1 (window undefined for the actual item; months to reach 20 per item; every two-courtroom morning red): (a) the estimate is no longer per item number. Each 'called' tap gives a pace r = elapsed sitting minutes / max(position, 3) for its (bench, segment), and the window for any item is the weighted q25-q75 pace times that item's position. Every call informs every item, so 20 observations per bench accrue in about a week at normal logging rates, with a pooled-segment fallback. (b) States are split. Red OVERLAP needs both windows estimated. Amber POSSIBLE CLASH, NO DATA is one notice per advocate-morning, offers no reassignment unless the advocate opts in, and same-courtroom items never clash. (c) test_cold_start_3_weeks seeds 10 advocates x 6 matters x 3 courtrooms from zero logs and asserts zero red and zero suggestions on day 1, red precision >= 0.8 by day 15, and at least 70% of mornings with no notice; test_amber_opt_in and test_same_courtroom_no_clash cover the rest. Objection 2 (estimator undefined as written; test doesn't say which N): the model is now fully specified. Position is the rank in the day's printed list, with connected matters counted once and deleted items skipped. Segment labels are kept separate. The clock is in sitting minutes excluding lunch. The pace floor is 3. The weighted quantile is defined as the first cumulative weight >= q with no interpolation. The projection is start + q x position, capped at rising. test_mixed_item_numbers compares the service against an independent reference implementation on calls at mixed positions, test_reach_window_positions recovers known generating quantiles, and test_connected_matters_one_call, test_lunch_excluded and test_position_floor pin down the definitions.

### Acceptance tests

- tests/test_schedule.py::test_mixed_item_numbers: 40 synthetic calls for one bench and segment at positions {2,3,5,7,12,19,25,33,41} with known elapsed times yield, for a target at position 8, a window equal to a separate reference implementation (tests/reference/weighted_quantile.py, written independently of the service: sort, cumulative weight, first >= q) to the minute.
- test_reach_window_positions: 100 calls spread over positions 1-60 with pace drawn from a known distribution give q25/q75 pace within 2% of the generating quantiles, and the window for position 30 is start + q x 30 in sitting minutes.
- test_position_floor: a call at item 1 after 4 minutes contributes pace 4/3, not 4.
- test_connected_matters_one_call: a list where item 8 is 'with 9-12' gives item 13 position 9, not 13.
- test_lunch_excluded: a projected window crossing 13:00 is shifted by the lunch interval; elapsed for a 14:30 call excludes 75 minutes.
- test_rising_cap: a position whose lower bound passes rising time reads 'may not be reached today' and is never red.
- test_pooled_fallback: a segment with W=8 on a bench with pooled W=30 uses the pooled pace and the 'pooled' label; pooled W=12 gives 'not enough data'.
- test_cold_start_3_weeks: seeded firm of 10 advocates, each with 6 matters in 3 courtrooms per sitting day over 15 sitting days, zero logs on day 1 and about 3 synthetic calls per courtroom per day after that. Day 1: zero red pairs, at most one amber notice per advocate-morning, zero reassignment suggestions. By day 15: every red pair is a true overlap in the synthetic ground truth with precision >= 0.8, and at least 70% of advocate-mornings have no notice at all.
- test_amber_opt_in: an amber morning yields reassignment suggestions only after the advocate's 'treat as clash' flag is set.
- test_same_courtroom_no_clash: two items for one advocate in the same courtroom never produce a clash state.
- test_clash_detection: two estimated windows in complexes 25 minutes apart that overlap after padding give red; non-overlapping ones give none; one estimated and one missing give amber.
- test_roster_change_resets_window, test_older_roster_downweighted (effective weight 10 + 0.5 x 30 = 25), test_unparsed_header: as in round 2, now asserted on pace observations.
- test_reassignment_first, test_reassignment_ranking, test_client_message_gate_criminal, test_client_message_gate_presence_order, test_client_message_template, test_client_message_needs_click, test_adjournment_counter, test_proxy_brief_quotes: carried over unchanged; 'adjourn' never appears in any suggestion over 200 random scenarios, including amber opt-in ones.
- test_no_ai_in_schedule: schedule_service makes zero Anthropic calls (mock asserts zero).

### Reviewers' minor notes

- Several High Courts and the Supreme Court publish live court display boards showing the item each courtroom is on. Where one exists, poll it as an observation source: it gives 'called' events for every item, not only the firm's own. That removes most of the junior-tapping work, largely fixes the cold start, and makes 'court rose at item K' automatic. Keep taps for courts with no display board.
- Pace is E / position in the full printed order, but it is pooled per segment. When the number of fresh matters ahead of the regular list changes a lot from day to day, the regular-segment pace will be biased. Consider measuring position from the start of the segment plus a per-segment offset, or at least test with a varying fresh-list length.
- Heavy-tailed regular or final-hearing lists (one part-heard matter taking the morning) will make q75 very wide. Show the window width and flag when it is too wide to be useful, rather than painting most mornings red.
- Migration number 016 is fine, but 014 is claimed by six other proposals. Coordinate the numbering.
- A reassignment candidate who knows the matter may still not be on record. An advocate who is not on the vakalatnama generally needs a memo of appearance or a fresh vakalatnama, depending on the forum's rules and on whether this is a proxy appearance or a substantive one. The candidate list should show 'on vakalatnama: yes/no'. For a 'no', the proxy brief should include a memo-of-appearance task, so the colleague is not turned away at the bar.
- Per-judge pace statistics should be contractually and technically barred from leaving the firm: no export, no cross-org pooling, no sharing with other firms. A shared 'judge speed' table would invite misuse and contempt-adjacent commentary. Say this in safeguards and add an RLS test showing org B cannot read org A's appearance_logs.
- Data dependency: it relies on P05 parsing bench headers and segment labels. High Court cause lists are public PDFs, but district-court cause lists on services.ecourts.gov.in sit behind a captcha that must never be bypassed. The court profile should mark district complexes as 'manual or eCourtsIndia API only', and the board should not imply coverage it doesn't have.
- test_cold_start_3_weeks scores precision against synthetic ground truth. If the generator uses the same linear pace model as the estimator, the test proves very little. Generate ground truth with non-linear effects (part-heard blocks, long first matters, a judge rising early) so precision >= 0.8 means something.
- Several High Courts and the Supreme Court publish live display boards showing the current item per court. Those boards are a lawful, cheaper source of 'called' observations than junior taps, and the morning view could correct itself from them. Worth adding as an optional input.
- Migration 016 is fine as a number, but six other proposals this round all claim 014. The numbering needs coordinating before any code is written.
